Large Quantity Electronics

A few devices and a carload are not always handled the same way. For a large quantity, contact the destination before arriving.

The rule is simple

One or a few items: ordinary published drop-off rules may be enough.

Carload or large quantity: contact the destination first.

Capacity, staffing, resident limits and program rules can change how a large load is handled.

Identify the load before you call

  • Household cleanout: computers, TVs, printers, cables, phones, and accessories accumulated at one residence.
  • Business or nonprofit replacement: multiple workstations, monitors, printers, servers, or networking equipment.
  • Mixed load: electronics combined with appliances, batteries, lamps, scrap metal, or ordinary trash.
  • Unusual equipment: oversized copiers, server racks, medical or scientific devices, damaged screens, or dismantled equipment.

These loads may use different prices, receiving areas, or recyclers. A mixed trailer should not be described simply as “a load of electronics.”

Call-ahead checklist

  • Who generated the load: household, business, nonprofit, or government?
  • How many TVs, monitors, computers, printers, and other devices?
  • Are any items over 100 pounds, dismantled, broken, leaking, or swollen?
  • Are batteries, lamps, appliances, or non-electronic materials mixed in?
  • What vehicle or trailer will arrive, and is an appointment required?
  • Is proof of residency, a municipal permit, or a fee required?
  • Does the service include data destruction, or only material recycling?

Business and nonprofit loads

New York’s manufacturer program defines “consumer” broadly to include individuals, businesses, nonprofits, schools, and government entities. Most consumers should not be charged for ordinary recycling of covered equipment through an applicable manufacturer program.

Manufacturers may charge for recycling from for-profit businesses with 50 or more full-time employees and nonprofits with 75 or more full-time employees. Any consumer may also be charged for premium services such as on-site collection, specialized packing, refurbishment, or data wiping.

Ask for a written description of the service when chain of custody, serial-number reporting, data destruction, or a recycling certificate matters.

What can change the answer

Quantity is only the starting point. Ownership type, employee count, equipment condition, data-security needs, vehicle size, residency, and mixed materials can all change eligibility or price.

Data is a separate job

Recycling a device does not automatically prove that data was erased. Decide whether you will erase or destroy storage yourself or pay for a documented data-security service before the load leaves your control.
