What Is — and Isn’t — E‑Waste?

Electronics-containing does not automatically mean e-waste. Batteries, microwaves, small appliances and vehicle components can follow completely different systems.

Covered electronic equipment in New York

New York’s recycling law uses the narrower term covered electronic equipment. The current state list includes:

  • Computers such as desktops, laptops, tablets, e-readers, smart displays, smart watches, and certain virtual-reality headsets
  • Computer peripherals such as monitors, keyboards, mice, external hard drives, and qualifying printers or scanners under 100 pounds
  • Small electronic equipment such as game consoles, DVD players, DVRs, VCRs, converter boxes, and cable or satellite receivers
  • Small-scale servers and televisions over four inches

Covered equipment may include a cable, cord, or wiring that accompanies the device.

Common items outside that state-law list

“Not covered by the Act” does not mean “put it in the trash.” It means the item may use a different recycling or disposal system. State examples outside the covered-equipment list include cameras, phones, radios, GPS receivers, calculators, thermostats, vehicle parts, and household appliances.

  • Phones: not covered electronic equipment under this Act, but wireless providers have a separate New York collection requirement and local programs may accept them.
  • Cameras and GPS units: excluded from the covered list, but UCRRA’s local program may accept them.
  • Vehicle traction batteries: automotive/end-of-life system, not ordinary consumer e-waste.
  • Refrigerant appliances: appliance route with special handling, not an ordinary computer-and-TV drop-off.

Legal category and local acceptance are different tests

First ask which legal or material category the item belongs to. Then ask whether the specific destination accepts it. A local program can accept more than the state covered-equipment list, and a facility can also limit items that are covered under state law.

For example, UCRRA lists cameras and GPS/navigation devices even though those products are outside the state covered-equipment definition. Conversely, a municipal transfer station may reject all electronics and direct residents to UCRRA or a manufacturer program.

Use this two-question test

  1. What is the item? Computer, display, peripheral, small electronic equipment, battery, appliance, lamp, vehicle component, or another category?
  2. What does the destination say? Confirm the exact item, size, condition, quantity, eligibility, season, and fee.

If the classification is unclear, NY DEC’s E-waste Recycling Team lists 518-402-8706 and ewaste@dec.ny.gov for questions.

Why the distinction matters

New York bans covered electronic equipment from trash and curbside trash pickup. Other electronic products may have separate take-back laws, recycling programs, or facility rules. Correct classification prevents a wasted trip and keeps batteries or appliances out of the wrong collection stream.
